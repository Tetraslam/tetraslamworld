# Things I find beautiful: collecting guide

The page is a visual collection with room for personal observations. An entry can celebrate a detail without endorsing the entire thing. No review score or obligatory explanation is required.

## Workflow

Open **admin → taste → add entry**. New entries start as drafts.

1. Give the entry a specific title. “The rear-wheel housing on the Citroën DS” is as legitimate as “Citroën DS.”
2. Optionally write the one thing you noticed. This short observation appears on the index.
3. Select whole/detail and add context, maker, and period when useful.
4. Add ordered media. Pick a cover; use a crop or still that directs attention. Add alternate views and context after it.
5. Add source and credit links separately. Clicking the index image opens your entry; source links open the original.
6. Pick categories (what it is) and qualities (what you notice). Old tags remain available as additional search words.
7. Preview, then publish. Drag the unfiltered admin list to curate the order. “Give this entry more room” makes an entry wider on larger screens.

Edits are versioned. If another session changes an entry, the save is rejected and the draft is retained. Reloading the saved version explicitly discards that draft. Removing an entry does not delete storage files that might be shared elsewhere.

## Category prompts

- **Objects & products:** furniture, cameras, lamps, tools, watches, everyday objects.
- **Websites:** a whole site, one page, navigation, or a particular interaction.
- **Apps & software:** interfaces, creative tools, developer tools, operating systems.
- **Vehicles:** cars, bicycles, trains, aircraft, ships, dashboards, cabins.
- **Motion & interactions:** transitions, loading sequences, effects, gesture responses.
- **Shaders & generative art:** dithering, light, particles, procedural forms, simulations.
- **Architecture & interiors:** façades, staircases, libraries, workshops, courtyards, lighting.
- **Landscapes & public space:** gardens, plazas, waterfronts, paths, shade, gathering places.
- **Infrastructure:** stations, bridges, canals, power plants, networks, logistics.
- **Mechanisms & engineering:** watch movements, linkages, instruments, circuits, joints.
- **Typography & writing systems:** typefaces, inscriptions, lettering, calligraphy, scripts, conlang glyphs.
- **Maps & diagrams:** atlases, transit maps, scientific figures, fictional cartography, schematics.
- **Books & printed matter:** layouts, covers, bindings, manuals, newspapers, marginalia.
- **Identity & wayfinding:** signage, symbols, uniforms, liveries, visual identities.
- **Materials & craft:** glaze, joinery, weaving, patina, finishes, material transitions.
- **Fashion & costume:** silhouettes, tailoring, jewellery, footwear, character costumes.
- **Photography:** framing, light, colour relationships, photographic series.
- **Painting & sculpture:** paintings, drawings, sculpture, installations, composition.
- **Film & animation:** a shot, background, lighting, editing, title sequences.
- **Games & fictional worlds:** environments, menus, maps, architecture, worldbuilding through ordinary objects.
- **Sound & music:** timbre, arrangements, motifs, transitions, sound design, specific performances.
- **Movement & performance:** choreography, poi, gestures, physical comedy, athletic technique.
- **Nature:** branching, minerals, clouds, animal movement, microscopic forms.
- **Food & hospitality:** presentation, table settings, cafés, the choreography of service.
- **Ideas & explanations:** a proof, abstraction, elegant code, diagram, or lucid explanation.

The same prompts live beside the editor at `/admin/taste/guide`. They are suggestions rather than mandatory bins. New categories can be added under additional metadata.

## Qualities: a separate axis

Proportion, restraint, rhythm, legibility, warmth, material honesty, patina, mechanical elegance, colour, light, texture, precision, playfulness, spatial generosity, continuity, surprise.

Choose your own words. A quality such as restraint can connect a watch, website, and building. Keep these distinct from categories, and avoid filling tags for their own sake.

## Media

- **Images:** upload originals; dimensions are captured automatically. For external file URLs, supply dimensions so the layout can reserve the right shape. The index contains the whole image; extreme ratios receive a bounded frame. Enlarge images on the entry page to inspect them.
- **Video:** MP4/H.264 or WebM are reliable browser choices. Uploading extracts a still poster. Index previews start on deliberate pointer/focus intent or the play button, stay muted, stop offscreen/hidden, and are limited to a short passage. Reduced motion disables automatic previews. The entry has full native controls.
- **Audio:** no autoplay. Use MP3/WAV or another format supported by your browser; optionally add artwork. Start/end timestamps identify the passage you admire, and the full recording remains inspectable.
- **Speech and accessibility:** recordings can include a Markdown transcript; videos can also reference a WebVTT captions URL. Describe important visual details in the media description or caption.
- **Interactive:** add an HTTPS example URL and preferably a poster. Nothing is embedded until the visitor clicks. Known video/playground URLs are adapted; arbitrary examples use an opaque sandbox and may reject framing. The original link is always available.
- **Text:** Markdown for prose; code for exact whitespace; TeX math for equations. Math has bounded expansion and cannot inject HTML or load external resources.
- **Animated images:** uploaded GIFs receive a still poster. Mark externally hosted GIFs, APNGs, or animated WebP as animated and supply a still. Short video is usually easier to control.

Up to 24 media items, 100 MB per uploaded file, and 64,000 serialized characters per entry. Media binaries live in storage, not in the database record. Long recordings should be linked or cut to the relevant moment.

## Editorial habits

One excellent representative image usually beats five interchangeable views. A crop can be the cover, with the full object later for context. Captions can annotate each image or example. Descriptions and notes are optional. A broader design language can be one entry with several deliberately ordered examples.

Use the precise thing you noticed, not an invented general endorsement. Leave room for things you simply love.
